# Episode 1 Story Bible — THE WAIT

## Format
- **Series:** JUNGLE GENERAL / The Invisible Workers
- **Episode:** 1
- **Title:** THE WAIT
- **Target duration:** approximately 90 seconds
- **Format:** 9:16 vertical
- **Genre:** Nigerian medical thriller / social drama
- **Visual treatment:** photorealistic live-action cinematic realism

## Core Premise
**Everybody wants the result. Nobody sees what it takes to produce it.**

Episode 1 makes the invisible work of laboratory verification visible. The central tension is not merely that a result is delayed; it is that releasing a questionable result quickly can be worse than taking the time required to investigate it.

## Clinical Responsibility
A positive malaria finding supports malaria but does **not** by itself exclude bacterial sepsis. Scientist Obinna owns laboratory analytical verification and validation within the story. Dr. Talon owns clinical diagnosis and treatment.

## Canonical Causal Chain
Storm at Jungle General → SUV arrives at Emergency → family transfers Mr. Okoro to the bed → Dr. Talon assesses high fever/chills/weakness → malaria versus sepsis is considered → Chidi pressures for certainty → Talon distinguishes suspicion from knowing → purple-top EDTA sample reaches laboratory → Obinna reviews FBC against clinical history → mismatch/questionable result recognized → analyzer maintenance status checked → required maintenance/cleaning performed → approximately 20-minute delay accepted → FBC rerun → repeat compared with initial result → malaria film/microscopy progressed → parasites detected → repeat FBC + microscopy validated/released → Obinna contacts Dr. Talon.

## Sequence Map

### SEQ 01 — Storm / Jungle General
**Purpose:** establish urgency and the hospital identity.  
**Environment:** ancient root-and-rock/jungle exterior integrated with a functioning modern Nigerian hospital.  
**Continuity:** rain, lightning and wet exterior surfaces remain outside the protected clinical interior.

### SEQ 02 — Emergency Arrival
**Purpose:** move Mr. Okoro from arrival into care without losing urgency.  
**Action:** SUV reaches Emergency; family transfers him inside and onto the bed.  
**Known human-version asset:** `Jungle General: Stormy Emergency Entrance.png` establishes an inside-the-Emergency view looking outward toward the stormy driveway/SUV.

### SEQ 03 — Clinical Assessment
**Purpose:** establish symptoms and uncertainty.  
**Symptoms:** high fever, chills, weakness.  
**Clinical concern:** malaria versus sepsis.

Mrs. Okoro:
> “Doctor… what is happening to him?”

Dr. Talon:
> “High fever. Chills. Weakness.”

Chidi pressures for an answer.

Dr. Talon:
> “I know what I suspect. That is not the same as knowing.”

### SEQ 04 — Specimen to Laboratory
**Purpose:** shift audience attention from visible bedside care to invisible diagnostic work.  
**Prop:** purple-top EDTA specimen.  
**Destination:** Specimen Reception / diagnostic laboratory workflow.

### SEQ 05 — Questionable FBC
**Purpose:** introduce the laboratory decision point.  
Obinna reviews the initial FBC in the context of the clinical history and identifies a mismatch/questionable result. He does not release it simply because the analyzer generated numbers.

### SEQ 06 — Maintenance / The Delay
**Purpose:** make the title “The Wait” meaningful.  
Obinna checks analyzer maintenance status and performs required maintenance/cleaning. He accepts an explicit delay of approximately **20 minutes** rather than validate a result he does not trust.

### SEQ 07 — Rerun / Comparison
**Purpose:** demonstrate verification rather than blind machine acceptance.  
The FBC is rerun and the repeat is compared with the initial result.

### SEQ 08 — Malaria Microscopy
**Purpose:** progress the investigation beyond the questionable automated result.  
Malaria-film microscopy is performed/progressed and parasites are detected.

### SEQ 09 — Validation / Communication
**Purpose:** complete the laboratory responsibility chain.  
Obinna validates/releases the repeat FBC and microscopy findings and contacts Dr. Talon.

## Episode Meaning
The laboratory delay is reframed as professional judgment and patient-safety work. The machine is part of the process; it is not the final authority.

## Exclusions
- No animal allegory.
- No paw-print or “Wildlife Care” branding.
- No fantasy medicine.
- No steampunk/sci-fi laboratory.
- No implication that malaria positivity automatically rules out sepsis.
- No portrayal of Chidi as the patient.
- No insertion of later-episode hierarchy dialogue unless canon is explicitly revised.
