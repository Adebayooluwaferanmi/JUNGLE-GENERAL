# Episode 1 — The Wait

## Logline
When a seriously ill Mr. Okoro arrives at Jungle General with a high fever, the family's frightening wait collides with the invisible responsibility of Scientist Obinna, who refuses to release a questionable blood result until the analyzer is checked, the test is rerun and microscopy provides the evidence the clinical team needs.

## Core Perspective
The episode makes invisible laboratory work visible. It contrasts what waiting feels like outside the laboratory with what responsible verification requires inside it.

## Principal Cast
Scientist Obinna · Dr. Talon · Mr. Okoro · Mrs. Okoro · Chidi Okoro

Dr. Adewale belongs to the wider series canon and is not to be inserted into Episode 1 without an approved story beat.

## Locked Medical Progression
### Presentation
Mr. Okoro presents weak, sweating, febrile and shivering. Mrs. Okoro and Chidi accompany him.

### Clinical Assessment
Dr. Talon assesses the patient and considers **malaria vs sepsis**.

### Laboratory Entry
A purple-top EDTA specimen enters the FBC workflow.

### Discrepancy
Obinna sees an initial FBC result that is inconsistent with the clinical picture.

### Verification
Rather than validate questionable data, Obinna checks analyzer maintenance/status and performs the required maintenance/cleaning.

### The Wait
This introduces an explicit **approximately 20-minute delay**. The delay is a dramatic device grounded in responsible laboratory practice.

### Repeat
The FBC is rerun and compared.

### Microscopy
Malaria-film microscopy is performed/requested and parasites are detected.

### Validation
The repeat FBC and microscopy findings are correlated, validated and released.

### Communication
Obinna communicates the laboratory outcome to Dr. Talon.

### Clinical Responsibility
Dr. Talon retains diagnosis/treatment responsibility. Malaria positivity supports malaria but does not automatically exclude bacterial sepsis.

## Narrative Guardrails
- Do not make Obinna careless to manufacture conflict.
- Do not make Dr. Talon incompetent; her tension with laboratory staff is interpersonal/hierarchical, not a substitute for medical competence.
- Do not imply the laboratory independently diagnoses/treats the patient.
- Do not imply malaria microscopy alone eliminates all sepsis concern.
- Do not turn Chidi into staff or the patient.
- Do not reintroduce animal allegory imagery.
