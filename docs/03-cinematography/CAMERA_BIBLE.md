# Camera Bible

## Shot Specification Order
1. physical camera placement
2. camera height
3. orientation / side of action
4. framing
5. lens intent
6. camera movement
7. subject movement
8. foreground/midground/background
9. focus behavior
10. required end frame

## Separate Camera Motion from Subject Motion
Specify them independently. “Camera dollies backward while the subject advances toward camera” is controllable; “dynamic cinematic movement” is not.

## 180° Rule
For continuous action establish:
- action axis
- eyelines
- screen direction
- entrances/exits
- relative left/right placement

Remain in the legal camera semicircle. Cross only through a deliberately designed neutral/reorienting shot or motivated move.

## Motion Budget
Avoid asking one generation to preserve multiple identities, manipulate several props, perform complex locomotion, execute elaborate camera movement and transform the environment simultaneously. Split complex choreography into coverage.

## Image-to-Video
The input image already defines static appearance. Prioritize:
- what begins moving
- direction/trajectory
- timing/order
- camera motion
- what remains locked
- exact end state

## Extend vs New Shot
Use temporal extension when the next beat genuinely continues the same camera setup and spatial state. Do **not** use Extend merely to obtain a new coverage angle if it causes composition/geography drift. A new angle should be designed as a new shot from the locked scene geography and references.

## Motion Language
Prefer measurable physical verbs: advances, retreats, turns, crosses frame left-to-right, lowers, raises, stops, settles, pans, tilts, dollies, trucks, cranes, orbits.
